## 1. SimGym in depth

### What it is
SimGym is a Shopify product, in **AI Research Preview**, that runs AI-driven "robot" shoppers through a merchant's actual storefront to test whether a theme/layout change helps or hurts. Shopify's own framing of the problem it solves: *"A merchant changes their storefront. New theme, different layout. How do they know if it is better? Deploy it, A/B test it, wait days or weeks for statistical significance."* [shopify.engineering/simgym](https://shopify.engineering/simgym) It exists because most Shopify merchants are small: *"typically they don't have enough traffic for testing to converge at all"* — SimGym gives them *"a simulated A/B test in minutes instead of weeks, even for stores with zero real traffic."* [shopify.engineering/simgym](https://shopify.engineering/simgym)

### How it works
- **Personas:** *"Each [robot] has a persona, a budget, and a shopping intent. Each runs in a cloud browser."* [shopify.engineering/simgym](https://shopify.engineering/simgym)
- **Real browsing, not mocked:** *"No mocked DOM, no shortcuts. The robot sees the page, decides what to do, clicks, scrolls, adds to cart."* [shopify.engineering/simgym](https://shopify.engineering/simgym)
- **The decision loop:** Browserbase (up to 2,000 concurrent Chromium sessions) sends page state to an inference cluster; *"the model returns a JSON action, Browserbase executes it. Repeat 10–13 times"* per session. The model input is *"a one-sentence system prompt, a buyer profile, accumulated memory, and a representation tree"* of the page. [shopify.engineering/simgym](https://shopify.engineering/simgym)
- **Model/infra:** `gpt-oss-120b` (open-weight MoE) on 48 dedicated NVIDIA B200 GPUs via CentML, 128K context, MXFP4 quantization, FlashInfer kernels, EAGLE-3 speculative decoding. Throughput reached 400,000 shopping sessions/day at single-digit dollars per merchant run and falling. [shopify.engineering/simgym](https://shopify.engineering/simgym)
- **What it measures:** primarily **add-to-cart ratio**, comparing an active theme against an unpublished/draft theme, plus qualitative feedback on navigation, checkout friction, and store layout. [help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym), [changelog.shopify.com](https://changelog.shopify.com/posts/shopify-simgym-is-now-available-in-ai-research-preview-for-all-eligible-merchants)

### "AI Research Preview" status
The changelog post states plainly: *"SimGym is now available to all eligible merchants with no waitlist required."* [changelog.shopify.com](https://changelog.shopify.com/posts/shopify-simgym-is-now-available-in-ai-research-preview-for-all-eligible-merchants) The help-center page labels it *"a Shopify app in AI Research Preview"* — i.e., shipped and usable, but still carrying an explicit accuracy disclaimer rather than being presented as a finished, guaranteed-accurate product. [help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym)

### What Shopify says it's FOR and NOT for
- **FOR:** screening theme/layout changes cheaply, especially for low-traffic stores that can't reach statistical significance the normal way; comparing exactly one change at a time (*"testing one change at a time to isolate what drives behavior differences"*). [help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym)
- **NOT for:** a substitute for real customer data. Verbatim: *"results might differ from actual buyer behavior."* [help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym) It is directional guidance, not a definitive conversion predictor, and is scoped to **Liquid storefronts only** — Hydrogen/headless stores are explicitly unsupported. [help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym)
- **UNVERIFIED:** the specific claim that Shopify tells merchants to follow up SimGym results with "accessibility testing, device testing, analytics, real-user research, and controlled production measurement" appeared in an initial aggregated search summary attributed to shopify.engineering, but a direct fetch of that page did not surface this exact list — the safer, confirmed claim is the weaker one above (validate against real user research before big decisions). Treat the specific 5-item list as unconfirmed phrasing, not a quote.

### What a Shopify engineer would consider credible vs. a shallow name-drop
A shallow "SimGym-inspired" pitch says the word "simulation" and shows a bar chart. A credible one visibly borrows SimGym's actual *method*: agents with declared identities acting against a real decision boundary, at a scale no human rehearsal could match, reported as a comparison a merchant can act on — with an explicit synthetic/real disclaimer, because SimGym itself carries one.

**Five concrete, honest echoes for the Gym:**
1. **Persona definitions, not just "N runs."** SimGym gives every robot *"a persona, a budget, and a shopping intent"* — name the Gym's haggler archetypes the same way (e.g. budget-constrained, price-anchored, loyalty-seeking, adversarial) rather than one undifferentiated random distribution. [shopify.engineering/simgym](https://shopify.engineering/simgym)
2. **Seeded, reproducible runs.** SimGym's engineering post is full of throughput/latency numbers that only make sense as a *repeatable* benchmark. PLAN.md already commits to a deterministic seed for the 300-haggler run (`§7`) — say that number out loud to a judge; it's the difference between "we ran it once and it looked good" and an actual test harness.
3. **A/B comparison of two configurations, not a single number.** SimGym's headline feature is literally comparing draft theme vs. active theme on add-to-cart ratio. [help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym) The Gym's closest equivalent — already in the plan — is the floor-slider-driven histogram against reference lines for list price, floor, and a blanket 20% sale (PLAN.md `§10.2`, `§16.3`). Frame it explicitly as "policy A vs. policy B," the same shape as SimGym's theme A vs. B.
4. **Report a conversion-like metric, not just "it works."** SimGym's currency is add-to-cart ratio; the Gym's is win rate, margin vs. counterfactual, and aged-units-cleared (PLAN.md `§7`). Naming these as the Gym's equivalent of an add-to-cart ratio is a one-sentence bridge a judge can follow instantly.
5. **Label synthetic vs. real, out loud, the way SimGym does.** SimGym's own docs volunteer *"results might differ from actual buyer behavior"* unprompted. [help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym) Saying the equivalent line about the Gym's 300 synthetic hagglers — clearly, in the Console UI, not just in the pitch — is exactly the kind of intellectual honesty that reads as "we understood SimGym," not just "we heard the name."

A sixth, bonus signal worth having ready if asked: SimGym explicitly does **not** test pricing or negotiation — it tests theme/UX. PLAN.md already prepared the correct answer to this (`§0` decision D-line): *"SimGym tests themes; the Gym tests pricing rules."* Do not claim SimGym said anything about pricing being "out of scope" — that specific quote could not be verified against any primary source and should stay dropped, exactly as PLAN.md already decided.

## 2. Shopify's 2026 strategic narrative on AI + commerce

### Headline themes
- **Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP)** — note the real name is **Commerce**, not "Cart." An open standard, co-developed by Shopify and Google, for how AI agents discover and transact with merchants end-to-end (cart, checkout, payment, post-purchase). Announced by Sundar Pichai at NRF 2026 (Jan 11, 2026), backed by Amazon, American Express, Etsy, Mastercard, Meta, Microsoft, Salesforce, Stripe, Target, Visa, and Walmart; ships under Apache 2.0 on GitHub. [shopify.engineering/UCP](https://shopify.engineering/UCP), [shopify.com/news/ai-commerce-at-scale](https://www.shopify.com/news/ai-commerce-at-scale) A narrower **Universal Cart API** (multi-brand cart aggregation, early access/waitlist) is a *sub-component* of UCP, not the whole protocol — don't conflate the two names in the pitch.
- **Agentic Storefronts** — debuted in the Winter '26 Edition (Dec 10, 2025); a command-center surface inside Shopify admin that syndicates a merchant's products to AI channels (ChatGPT, Copilot, Google AI Mode, Shop app), managed centrally. Rolled out to all stores by late March 2026. [shopify.com/news/winter-26-edition-agentic-storefronts](https://www.shopify.com/news/winter-26-edition-agentic-storefronts)
- **Shopify Catalog** — the structured product dataset that powers UCP/agent discovery. Spring '26 (June 17, 2026) added image search, multimodal queries, and bulk lookup up to 50 products/request, and made UCP access fully self-serve (no more approval committee). Headline stat: *"AI searches powered by Shopify Catalog convert at 2x the rate of those using scraped data."* [shopify.com/news/spring-26-edition-merchant](https://www.shopify.com/news/spring-26-edition-merchant)
- **Sidekick** — Shopify's admin-embedded AI assistant (launched Jan 2025), now able to build custom admin apps for merchants. **UNVERIFIED:** the widely-repeated "385% YoY weekly-active-shops growth, 12,000+ custom apps in a quarter" figure traces to Shopify's Q1 2026 earnings commentary but was only found via secondary aggregation (Medium/RetailBrew), not a directly-fetched Shopify investor-relations release — treat the number as directionally right but not primary-confirmed.
- **ChatGPT integration pivot** — worth knowing precisely, since it bears on §7: OpenAI walked back its original "Instant Checkout" (buy-in-chat) feature. OpenAI's own stated reason: *"the initial version of Instant Checkout did not offer the level of flexibility that we aspire to provide, so we're allowing merchants to use their own checkout experiences while we focus our efforts on product discovery."* Shopify's response is to route ChatGPT purchases through an **in-app browser** completing the merchant's own checkout, not a black-box in-chat buy button. [shopify.com/news/ai-commerce-at-scale](https://www.shopify.com/news/ai-commerce-at-scale) — this is directly relevant to Bazaar's own "haggle in ChatGPT, checkout on our own page" design; it matches, rather than fights, where Shopify itself is steering the ecosystem.
- **UCP and discounts, explicitly:** *"Shopify merchants are UCP-enabled by default, so any surface that builds with it incorporates a merchant's specific checkout rules, discounts, and customizations."* [shopify.com/news/spring-26-edition-merchant](https://www.shopify.com/news/spring-26-edition-merchant) The AI-commerce-at-scale piece adds that the platform supports *"discount codes, loyalty credentials, subscription billing options, and pre-order/final sale confirmations directly within chat conversations,"* and that customers can *"submit discount codes"* inside an agent conversation. [shopify.com/news/ai-commerce-at-scale](https://www.shopify.com/news/ai-commerce-at-scale) — i.e., a discount code settling in a chat conversation is not a hack the team invented; it's the exact primitive Shopify's own protocol was built to carry.

### Six quotable lines for the pitch (with source)
1. *"You remain the merchant of record with full ownership of the customer relationship and post-purchase experience."* — [shopify.com/news/winter-26-edition-agentic-storefronts](https://www.shopify.com/news/winter-26-edition-agentic-storefronts)
2. *"Every order flows into your admin, every customer stays yours."* — same page.
3. *"Your brand shows up the way you want it to: product details, pricing, and availability are accurate."* — same page.
4. *"We know how much your brand means to you and we will give you the tools to keep telling your story."* — Vanessa Lee, VP of Product, Shopify. — same page.
5. *"Shopify merchants are UCP-enabled by default, so any surface that builds with it incorporates a merchant's specific checkout rules, discounts, and customizations."* — [shopify.com/news/spring-26-edition-merchant](https://www.shopify.com/news/spring-26-edition-merchant)
6. *"You decide where you show up, see what's working, and fix what's not, all in the same admin you already use."* — same page.

Also notable, and a nice unplanned gift for a project literally codenamed Bazaar: UCP's own engineering post describes its namespace-governance model (no central approval committee, organizations own a reverse-domain namespace) as creating *"an open bazaar of capabilities, evolving as freely as commerce itself."* [shopify.engineering/UCP](https://shopify.engineering/UCP) Worth a knowing aside in the pitch if there's room.

### What Shopify has said that the team must not contradict
Shopify's own enterprise blog runs a piece specifically weighing the *risks* of dynamic/personalized pricing, and it is unambiguous about the danger of opaque, per-customer pricing:
- *"customers' dynamic pricing frustrations rose from 14% to 28% year over year"* (Thales, cited by Shopify). [shopify.com/enterprise/blog/...dynamic-pricing...](https://www.shopify.com/enterprise/blog/102104006-dynamic-pricing-the-art-and-black-magic-of-situational-pricing)
- Instacart's AI-pricing rollback is cited as the cautionary example, including Instacart's own remediation promise: *"if two families are shopping for the same items, at the same time, from the same store location on Instacart, they see the same prices — period."* — same page.
- A cited 2026 Talker Research finding: *"62% of respondents are concerned about retailers using personalized pricing,"* with roughly two-thirds saying they'd stop shopping with a brand caught using personal data to charge them more. — same page.
- Regulatory backdrop cited: New York's Algorithmic Pricing Disclosure Act requires disclosure of algorithmic pricing that uses personal consumer data; the piece notes *"over 100 state bills addressing price transparency were introduced across the US in 2025."* — same page.

**Implication for the pitch:** Bazaar's negotiation must be framed, out loud, as the *opposite* of the thing Shopify's own content warns against — it is not silent, data-driven, per-shopper price discrimination; it's a visible, opt-in "make an offer" mechanic any shopper can trigger, with the same floor and rules applying to everyone who negotiates, enforced in code the merchant set and can audit. The team should be ready to say exactly this if a judge raises the Instacart comparison — which a generalist judge who read Shopify's own blog plausibly will.

## 3. Shopify's own content on the discounting problem

Shopify's blog argues directly against the behavior Bazaar exists to fix — blanket, habit-forming discounting — and for the alternatives (bundling, targeted clearance) Bazaar leans on:

- **Margin math, stated plainly:** *"If an item that sells for $30 with a profit margin of $10 is discounted by 30%, the result is only $1 of profit."* [shopify.com/blog/no-discounting-strategy](https://www.shopify.com/blog/no-discounting-strategy)
- **Trains customers to wait:** *"When consumers know an item will eventually go on sale, they feel no urgency to buy it right away."* — same page.
- **Devalues the product itself**, citing The Economist: *"Consumers think a discounted item does not function as well as a full-priced item."* — same page.
- **A real, named cautionary tale:** JCPenney's 2012 attempt to eliminate discounts backfired because customers were already trained to wait — but the initial no-discount shift alone cost them: *"In the first quarter post-implementation of the new pricing strategy, JCPenney lost $163 million"*, on the way to bankruptcy. — same page. (Useful nuance for the pitch: the lesson isn't "never discount," it's "don't let customers set the terms of when and how much" — exactly what Bazaar's code-enforced floor claims to do instead.)
- **Bargain hunters are the wrong customers to chase:** they *"are often brand agnostic and unlikely to convert into loyal shoppers."* — same page.
- **Cart abandonment, with real numbers merchants live with:** 70.19% of carts abandoned overall (2024 study cited); mobile abandonment 85% vs. desktop 73%; top cause is unexpected costs at 47%; the cited Baymard figure for the annual cost is *"ecommerce stores lose $260 billion in recoverable sales revenue each year because of cart abandonment."* [shopify.com/blog/shopping-cart-abandonment](https://www.shopify.com/blog/shopping-cart-abandonment)
- **Bundling as the sanctioned alternative to blanket discounting:** Shopify's own bundling piece backs this with named merchant results — Lvly saw *"a 6% year-over-year increase in average order value"* after expanding bundles on Shopify Plus (2023); HiSmile's bundled products became over 80% of orders and quadrupled average cart size. [shopify.com/blog/bundling-for-retail](https://www.shopify.com/blog/bundling-for-retail)
- **Aged inventory's carrying cost, in Shopify's own words:** *"the carrying costs of aged inventory ultimately lead to diminished gross margins: the longer the stock sits, the more expensive it becomes by preventing you from bringing in new products that generate cash flow."* Shopify's own two recommended fixes are exactly bundling and discounting the aging item. [shopify.com/blog/aged-inventory](https://www.shopify.com/blog/aged-inventory) (Shopify's obsolete-inventory piece confirms the qualitative framing — *"ties up capital"*, liquidation *"unlocks cash"* — but, checked directly, does **not** give a quantified dollar/percentage figure for the cost; don't invent one. [shopify.com/blog/obsolete-inventory](https://www.shopify.com/blog/obsolete-inventory))

**Pitch-ready synthesis:** every plank of Bazaar's premise — blanket discounts erode margin and train customers to wait, bundling raises AOV without a naked discount, aged stock has a real carrying cost that clearing helps — is stated by Shopify's own blog, in Shopify's own numbers, independent of anything the team is claiming. That's a stronger foundation than any market stat the team could bring in from outside.

## 6. Red flags for Shopify judges, and relevant ToS/API terms

### What plausibly marks a project down (judgement, informed by §§1–5 above, not a single cited source)
- **A generic chatbot with no merchant angle.** The 2026 brief's own Impact Potential criterion demands the project *"meaningfully improves merchant operations or customer experience"* — a shopping assistant with no visible connection to a real merchant's margin, inventory, or policy fails this criterion by the brief's own wording. [hackthenorth2026.devpost.com](https://hackthenorth2026.devpost.com/)
- **Fake/mocked data instead of real Admin API calls.** Given the brief explicitly invites use of *"Shopify's ecosystem (Shopify API, Remix, Polaris design system, and more,"* a judge with engineering background will plausibly probe whether numbers shown (cost, stock age, margin) are live from a real store or hardcoded — this is exactly why Bazaar's plan insists on real `unitCost`/stock-age sync rather than fixture data (PLAN.md `§0`, `§10.1`).
- **Claims about changing Shopify's own checkout.** Directly relevant to a real contractual line: the Shopify API Terms of Use state, *"not, except with Shopify's express written authorization, use an alternative to Shopify Checkout for checkout or payment processing"* (Section 2.3.18, as paraphrased from the fetched terms). [shopify.com/legal/api-terms](https://www.shopify.com/legal/api-terms) Bazaar settles through a real discount code into Shopify's own checkout, which is squarely compliant — but the pitch should never imply the team modified or bypassed Shopify Checkout itself; frame every mention as "we settle *into*" checkout, not "we built our own checkout."
- **Scraping.** Section 2.3.14 of the same terms bars *"any systematic or automated data collection activities (including scraping, data mining, data extraction and data harvesting)"* via the Shopify API. [shopify.com/legal/api-terms](https://www.shopify.com/legal/api-terms) Not applicable to Bazaar's design (it reads its own store's Admin API, which is the intended use), but worth being ready to distinguish if asked — "we call our own store's Admin API with our own token" is not scraping.
- **Per-customer price discrimination.** No clause explicitly bans this in the fetched API Terms — the closer risk is reputational/UX, not contractual: see §2's Instacart discussion. The distinguishing line for the pitch: Bazaar's floor and negotiation rules are the *same for every shopper who chooses to haggle* (an opt-in mechanic, not a covert per-person price set from personal data), which is the opposite of the pattern Shopify's own enterprise blog warns against.
- **Discount-code misuse.** No clause found banning automated/programmatic discount-code *creation* via the Admin API — `discountCodeBasicCreate` is the documented, intended mechanism for exactly this. [shopify.dev — discountCodeBasicCreate](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/mutations/discountcodebasiccreate) The real-world risk category ("discount code abuse") that does exist in Shopify's own ecosystem discourse is about codes *leaking* and being reused beyond their intended shopper — which is precisely why PLAN.md's single-use, TTL'd, per-session code design (`§0`, `§6.1`) is the right defensive posture to narrate if a judge asks about abuse.

### Clauses actually found, with exact text
- **Section 2.3.18** (checkout alternative): *"not, except with Shopify's express written authorization, use an alternative to Shopify Checkout for checkout or payment processing."* [shopify.com/legal/api-terms](https://www.shopify.com/legal/api-terms)
- **Section 2.3.14** (scraping): *"not use the Shopify API to conduct any systematic or automated data collection activities (including scraping, data mining, data extraction and data harvesting)"* — plus a requirement to request only the minimum data necessary. [shopify.com/legal/api-terms](https://www.shopify.com/legal/api-terms)
- **Section 2.3.6** (interference): *"not engage in any activity that interferes with, disrupts, harms, damages, or accesses in an unauthorized manner the servers, security, networks, data, applications or other properties or services of Shopify."* [shopify.com/legal/api-terms](https://www.shopify.com/legal/api-terms)
- **Section 2.4** (rate limits): *"Shopify may set and enforce limits on your use of the Shopify API... in our sole discretion."* [shopify.com/legal/api-terms](https://www.shopify.com/legal/api-terms)
- **Partner Program Agreement, on pricing transparency (for apps generally, not specific to negotiation features):** *"transparent and fair pricing for merchants... advance notice before adjusting any prices"* — this governs an *app's own pricing to the merchant* (i.e., what a Shopify Partner charges a store for using their app), not a merchant's own customer-facing pricing, so it is **not directly applicable** to Bazaar's negotiation feature, but is worth knowing so the team doesn't conflate the two if asked. **UNVERIFIED** at exact-clause level — this phrasing came from a secondary FAQ page (help.shopify.com/en/partners/help-support/faq/ppa) summarizing PPA changes rather than the PPA's own text being directly fetched and quoted.
- **No clause found, in either document fetched, that specifically addresses negotiated or personalized end-customer pricing** — the closest relevant material is Shopify's own *editorial* caution (§2 above), not a contractual restriction.

## 7. The verdict (~120 words)

Lead with **(b)** — the make-an-offer shopkeeper on the merchant's own storefront, with a SimGym-style Gym behind it — because Shopify's 2026 brief explicitly rewards *technical* surprise on *merchant* impact, and the floor-enforcement/Gym story is the harder, more verifiable claim. Use ChatGPT as the second beat, framed as "and because it's built on real Admin data and code-enforced rules, not a prompt, it travels" — which also happens to match where Shopify itself is steering agentic checkout (in-app-browser into the merchant's real checkout, not a black-box in-chat buy button). The 20-second moment: hand a judge the keyboard, let them try to talk the shopkeeper below the floor — and fail, live, in front of them.
